# Offers lens
Score: 5/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **Low effort and fast results.** Of the four value-equation terms, time and effort are where a can does well: you open it and feel it within minutes, with no habit to build and no skill to learn. Most offers fail on effort, and this one does not.
- **A dream outcome buyers already want.** "Energy without the synthetic stuff" is a clear, emotional promise aimed at people who already buy energy drinks. The lens prefers this to having to teach a market that a need exists.
- **Buyers with money and the habit of paying.** Energy-drink buyers already pay per can, often. A premium of a few crowns is a small decision for them, not a budget decision.
- **Room in the price at D2C.** From the brief's own numbers: 55 CZK retail minus 24 CZK cost leaves 31 CZK (56% of price). At 79 CZK it leaves 55 CZK (70%). The first batch costs about 72,000 CZK to produce (3,000 × 24) and, if every can sold direct at 55 CZK, would bring in 165,000 CZK before shipping, packaging, payment fees and marketing. That is enough room to fund a guarantee and bonuses, at least in the direct channel.
- **A cheap, bounded test.** A batch of 3,000 cans is a small, real-priced experiment rather than a bet on the company.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Likelihood: the brief gives nothing a buyer could believe yet.** The offer promises *energy*, but the brief does not give the caffeine per can. A mainstream energy-drink buyer will judge the can on whether it works as well as what they drink now. If it kicks noticeably less, "natural" turns from a benefit into an excuse, and nothing else in the stack can fix that. There is also no proof yet: no reviews, no buyers who came back for more, no tests against a familiar energy drink.
2. **Commodity drift into a category that already exists.** "Natural, clean-label, carbonated yerba mate" names a category, and does not set the can apart. If other mate or natural-energy cans already sit within reach of the same buyer (open question for /founder-competitors), the only levers left are price and design, and this lens says competing on price is how an offer dies. Right now the offer is the can itself, with no stack, no guarantee and no reason that makes saying no feel stupid.
3. **The margin disappears in the channels that bring volume.** The 31 CZK is a D2C figure. Cafés, distributors and chains each take a cut, and the brief gives no wholesale price or distributor margin. Cost is 24 CZK including VAT the company cannot reclaim, so any channel where the shelf price has to carry VAT, a retailer margin and a distributor margin could leave little or nothing for yerby at 55 CZK. D2C single cans also carry shipping costs per order that may be larger than the margin unless buyers order multipacks. If the market is only reachable profitably through D2C bundles, it is narrower than "mainstream energy-drink consumers". The real figures come from /founder-cfo.

Also weak on the market screen: the pain is a *preference* ("I'd rather not drink synthetics"), not a severe pain, and whether this segment is growing in Czechia is unknown (/founder-competitors, /founder-consumer).

## The questions I would ask the founder
1. How much caffeine is in one can, and how does a buyer feel it compared with the energy drink they drink today? Can you show it in one line on the can?
2. Who exactly is the first buyer: the student, the shift worker, the gym-goer or the office worker? Which of them has the strongest pain about synthetic ingredients and the money to pay 55–79 CZK, again and again?
3. Why would they believe it works on the first sip, before they have tried it? What proof can you get from the first 3,000 cans (tastings, repeat orders, café reorders)?
4. What is the guarantee? For example: "If it doesn't beat your usual energy drink, we refund the pack." Can the D2C margin carry that?
5. What is the wholesale price to a café and to a distributor, and what does yerby keep per can in each channel after the VAT it cannot reclaim? (/founder-cfo)
6. Could you sell at 79 CZK and still be the obvious choice? If not, what would have to be added (pack size, subscription, bundle) to make it so?
7. What is the minimum order quantity and the shelf life? Do they make the urgency real, or create a risk that stock goes out of date?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **State the energy claim and back it up.** Publish the caffeine per can and get evidence from tastings that target buyers find it works at least as well as their current drink. If it does not, change the recipe or the target buyer before the second batch.
2. **Turn the can into an offer.** Build a D2C stack, for example a multipack or subscription with a "beats your energy drink or your money back" guarantee, a clear name, and real scarcity from the limited first batch of 3,000 numbered cans. The price of the stack must be defended by value, not by being cheaper.
3. **Show a positive margin per can in every planned channel** before pursuing wholesale or retail, using real wholesale and distributor terms (/founder-cfo). If only D2C and direct café sales are profitable, start with those.
4. **Confirm the gap and the buyer.** /founder-competitors must show the can is not just one more natural mate can within reach of the same buyer, and /founder-consumer must show the named first buyer will pay at least 55 CZK and buy again.
