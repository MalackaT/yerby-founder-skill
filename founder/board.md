# Board meeting: yerby

## The vote
**2 FUND IF, 1 PASS.** Average score **4.7 / 10**.

| Lens | Score | Vote |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product | 6 | FUND IF |
| Offers | 5 | FUND IF |
| Monopoly | 3 | PASS |

The board likes the product and is unconvinced by the business around it. All three members call the first batch a cheap, bounded test (about 72,000 CZK at cost: 3,000 × 24 CZK), and that is the reason the PASS is easy to overturn rather than final.

## Risks raised by more than one member
1. **The energy claim is unproven (Product, Offers; Monopoly asks the same question).** The target is mainstream energy-drink drinkers, but caffeine per can is not stated, there is no added sweetener, and unsweetened mate tastes earthy. If the first can feels weaker or less tasty than the drink it replaces, "natural" becomes an excuse rather than a benefit. *Answered by:* a measured caffeine figure, and blind tastings → `/founder-consumer`.
2. **The margin in the volume channels is unknown (all three).** The ~31 CZK gap (56%) at 55 CZK exists only in direct sales, before shipping, payment fees and marketing. Cafés, distributors and chains each take a cut. The ~24 CZK cost includes VAT that yerby cannot reclaim, and the wholesale price and distributor terms aren't given. Shipping single 330 ml cans may cost more than the margin. *Answered by:* `/founder-cfo`.
3. **Four channels is too many for two founders and 3,000 cans (Product, Monopoly).** D2C, cafés, distributors and chains are four different prices and experiences. Spreading the batch across them teaches you nothing well. Both members want one beachhead.
4. **Commodity risk (Offers, Monopoly).** The recipe is good craft, not a secret. A contract producer or an incumbent could make a near-identical "natural" can. Unless the positioning is empty within reach of this buyer, only price and design are left. *Answered by:* `/founder-competitors`.
5. **No proof of repeat purchase yet (all three).** No tastings, no reorders, no reviews.

Raised by one member, but it blocks retail: **English-only labels and untested shelf life** (Product). Czech mandatory label information, and how taste and fizz hold up at months 1, 3 and 6 on a preservative-free drink, have to be settled before any distributor talk → `/founder-ops`.

## Conditions (the checklist for the rest of the pack)
- [ ] **Measure and state the caffeine per can**, and show how it compares with the energy drink the target buyer drinks now.
- [ ] **Prove the first sip.** Run blind tastings with real mainstream energy-drink drinkers (not mate fans or friends). Most should want a second can, or change the recipe or the target buyer before the second batch → `/founder-consumer`.
- [ ] **Name the beachhead.** Pick one small market (a set of venues, one city, one buyer type) and sell the first 3,000 cans into one channel the founders control end to end.
- [ ] **Show a positive margin per can in each channel you pursue**, using the real wholesale price, distributor terms, shipping per order and the non-reclaimable VAT. Start only with the channels that pass → `/founder-cfo`.
- [ ] **Show it isn't a commodity.** Either nobody owns this position within reach of the buyer, or there's a concrete moat plan: a brand built in one place first, exclusive venues, or a process the producer won't reuse → `/founder-competitors`.
- [ ] **Turn the can into an offer.** A D2C multipack or subscription, a guarantee along the lines of "beats your energy drink or your money back", and real scarcity from the numbered first batch. The price is defended by value, not by being cheaper → `/founder-offer`, `/founder-pricing`.
- [ ] **Prove repeat purchase from the first batch**: café reorders and returning D2C buyers.
- [ ] **Fix the gaps before retail**: Czech label compliance and a tested shelf life → `/founder-ops`.
- [ ] **One enemy, one sentence.** Narrow the social voice to energy drinks. Fighting matcha too blurs the message.

## Where the members disagree
- **Is there a business here at all?** Product (6) and Offers (5) see a good product with fixable gaps. Monopoly (3) sees a well-made can aimed at the most crowded part of the shelf, with no secret and nothing to stop a copycat. Its conditions (beachhead, moat, repeat buying) are stricter than the others'.
- **Where the value comes from.** Offers says value comes from the stack around the can: guarantee, bundles, scarcity. Product says it comes from the can and the first sip, and that a stack can't save a drink that disappoints. Monopoly says neither lasts without a defensible position.
- **Pricing.** Offers sees room to test 79 CZK if the offer is built up. Monopoly warns that against big incumbents, the margin may not survive the channel cuts at any price.

## The strongest version the board can see
This version is narrower than the pitch. The pitch is "the natural energy drink for mainstream Czech energy-drink consumers", sold across four channels. The board's strongest version is **the clean energy can of one place first**. For example: the default can in a few dozen cafés, gyms or offices in one city, which the founders stock and serve themselves, plus a D2C multipack or subscription to the email list, with a caffeine figure on the can that a Red Bull or Monster drinker trusts, and a guarantee that it works. The 3,000 numbered first-run cans are spent buying three facts: whether energy-drink drinkers want a second can, whether venues reorder, and what yerby keeps per can after the channel cut. If all three come back positive, the brand built in that beachhead is the moat, and wholesale and chains come after, on proven numbers. If the first sip fails with mainstream energy-drink drinkers, the fallback is a smaller but real market: buyers who already want natural, premium drinks, at a higher price.
