# Pricing: yerby

All prices in CZK, consumer prices with no VAT on top (yerby is a non-VAT payer). Sources: `pricing-curve.md` (the whole panel), `pricing-buyers.csv` (the 28 panel buyers only), `competitors.md`, and `numbers.json` run through the CFO tool. The panel answers are simulated. They tell you what to test, not what's proven.

## 1. The price: **59 CZK for a single can**, about 54 CZK per can in packs

| evidence | what it says |
| --- | --- |
| Whole panel (100) | Acceptable range **25–59**, IPP 40, median "getting expensive" at 50 |
| The 28 who buy | Acceptable range **30–65**, IPP 42, median "getting expensive" at 55, "too expensive" at 75. None of them call 55 too expensive |
| Label readers (14) and trend chasers (8), the best groups | Median "getting expensive" 55–59, "too expensive" 75–77 |
| Gym (14) and daily energy-drink regulars (16) | "Too expensive" at 60–65. 12 of 14 gym buyers and 10 of 16 regulars say no at 69 |
| Competitors (`competitors.md`) | Natural shelf 39.90–75.90, median 52.90. YEAHRBA 39.90, Club-Mate 56.90, ChariTea 66.90, Amity matcha 73.90 |
| CFO tool, base ramp | 55 → 17.62 a can, break-even 568 cans a month, year 1 +42,103 · **59 → 21.52 a can, break-even 465 cans a month, year 1 +77,983** · 65 → 27.38, 366, +131,895 · 49 → 11.77, 850, **−11,717** |

**Why 59 for a single can.** It sits at the top of the whole panel's acceptable range and well inside the buyers' range. It's level with Club-Mate and below ChariTea and Amity. It also adds 3.90 CZK of contribution per can over 55, about 36,000 CZK in year 1. A single can is bought for a trial, in a café or as an impulse, where buyers are least price-sensitive and per-order costs are highest.

**Why not 69–79 for now.** The people who would pay it (label readers, trend chasers) are 22 of 100. At 69, 6 of the 28 buyers and 12 of 14 gym buyers drop out. 79 is above every competitor found. The CFO's 69/79 scenarios assume volume holds, and the panel says it won't. Revisit after real pre-sale data.

**Why not under 50.** At 49 CZK, year 1 loses money (−11,717). Matching YEAHRBA's 39.90 would leave about 6 CZK a can, and break-even would rise above 1,500 cans a month. Don't compete on price.

## 2. The ladder

| rung | price | per can | why step up |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| **Single can** (cafés, tastings, e-shop add-on) | 59 | 59.00 | Trial. The panel's no. 1 ask is "let me try one can" |
| **6-pack** (e-shop) | 329 | 54.83 | Enough to know if you like it; carton cost spread over 6 |
| **12-pack** (e-shop, free shipping) | 649 | 54.08 | Best value per can; replaces a week or two of energy drinks |
| **Monthly 12-pack subscription** | 609 | 50.75 | Cheapest per can, delivered monthly, cancel anytime. Repeat buyers cost no new marketing |

The blended realised price stays close to 55 CZK a can if most volume ships in 12-packs, so `numbers.json` keeps **55** as the base. ESTIMATE: in a 12-pack at 54.08, contribution is about 16.70 a can. For subscribers it's about 18.90, because the 5 CZK marketing line mostly falls away on repeat orders. Check this with real numbers once orders come in.

**Cafés and bistros (B2B):** suggest a 59–65 shelf price and offer **38–40 wholesale**. The CFO estimates that leaves about 12.50–14.50 a can. That's thin, so use cafés for tastings, visibility and the brand, not as the main profit channel.

## 3. The opening offer: the numbered First Run

- **"First Run 12": the first 3,000 cans, each numbered**, sold as a 12-pack at **599 CZK** (49.92 a can) with free shipping. Sold **only until 31 January 2027, or until the first run sells out**, whichever comes first.
- This is a real launch price with an end date. It doesn't show a fake "was" price, and after the end date the 12-pack is 649.
- Plus **free tasting cans** at partner cafés and pop-ups during the launch month. The panel's no. 1 flip was "a free sample proves it isn't bitter". The cost of these cans is in the CFO's 5 CZK marketing line.
- ESTIMATE: at 49.92 a can, contribution is about 12.70. Over 3,000 cans that's about 15,000 CZK less than at 54.08. That's affordable as a one-off, but don't extend it.

## 4. What to test with real buyers

1. **Single-can price: 55 vs 65.** At café tastings or pop-ups, alternate the price by day (or by café) and count sales per hour of foot traffic. 65 is the buyers' PME, and 55 is the current target.
2. **12-pack: 599 vs 699 pre-sale.** Run two landing pages with the same copy and only the price different, split the email list 50/50 and use small paid ads. A real pre-order (paid, or a card saved) beats a "notify me" signup. Decide after about 100 visits per page.

Put the grams of sugar per can on both pages. Of the 100 buyers, 19 mentioned sugar, and several made it a condition for paying more than a Monster.

## 5. The panel's price objections (verbatim, simulated, for marketing)

1. *"It's 10 crowns more than a Red Bull for less drink, and it's not where I actually buy my energy drinks."* (P006)
2. *"The price per can. I buy multipacks on discount, and a single 55 CZK can from an e-shop with delivery is the opposite of how I shop."* (P011)
3. *"It says fruit concentrate but doesn't tell me how much sugar is in it. If I'm paying more than a Monster I want to see zero or close to zero sugar on the label, and it's not there."* (P073)

The answers are value per can (multipacks), being sold where they shop, and a printed sugar figure. Price cuts don't answer any of these.
