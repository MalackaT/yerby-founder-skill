# Monopoly lens
Score: 3/10 | Vote: PASS

The Monopoly lens asks whether this can become the dominant player in some market, however small, and stay there. As briefed, yerby is a well-made product aimed at the most crowded part of the drinks shelf, and it has no stated secret, no 10x advantage and no named small market to own first. That is a PASS today. It is a cheap PASS to overturn: the first batch costs about 72,000 CZK at cost (3,000 x 24 CZK), so the founders can buy the evidence below without betting the company.

## What is strong
- **The brand is the one plausible moat.** Of the four durable advantages (proprietary technology, network effects, scale, brand), brand is the only one a small drinks company can realistically build. The minimalist matte can and the provocative "natural vs synthetic energy" voice are a real start on it.
- **The product is retail-ready and the cost of a test is small.** Recipe, can design, EAN-13 barcode and a contract producer are in place. The first run of up to 3,000 cans is a bounded experiment, not a factory.
- **There is room in the price.** At 55 CZK retail against about 24 CZK landed cost, the gap is about 31 CZK a can (about 56% of the shelf price) before any retailer, distributor or shipping cut. At 79 CZK the gap is about 55 CZK (about 70%). Whether that survives the channel margins is an open question for /founder-cfo.
- **The timing argument is plausible.** "Clean label" and "no synthetic ingredients" give a why-now story, though the brief has no evidence yet that Czech energy-drink buyers are switching for that reason (/founder-consumer).

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **It is aimed at a big, crowded market instead of a small one it can own.** "Mainstream energy-drink consumers in Czechia" means fighting large incumbents for shelf space, fridge placement and price, plus whatever natural, mate and functional drinks already sell within reach of the same buyer. The lens says competition destroys profit. Who those rivals are and what they charge is an open question for /founder-competitors. Until a beachhead is named (for example a specific set of cafés, gyms, offices or one city), there is no market where yerby starts with a big share.
2. **There is no secret and nothing stops a copycat in month six.** The ingredients are water, cold-brewed yerba mate and natural citrus concentrates, made by a contract producer. That is good craft, not proprietary know-how: the same producer, or any other, could make a near-identical can, and an incumbent could launch a "natural" line with far more distribution. On the engineering question it is an incremental improvement, not a breakthrough.
3. **Distribution is undefined and unpriced.** The plan lists four channels (D2C, cafés and bistros, wholesalers, chains) but not how the first 100 customers arrive or what each one costs. An email funnel and founder posts are a start, not a channel. With a single 330 ml can, D2C shipping may eat the margin, and wholesale and chain margins are not given. Customer acquisition cost and channel margins are open questions for /founder-consumer and /founder-cfo. A good product with no way to reach buyers is not a business.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What is the one small market you could own most of within 12 months: which venues, which city, which type of buyer? Why that one first?
- What do you know about this drink or this buyer that the big energy brands and the existing natural-drink makers do not?
- If your contract producer, or a large incumbent, launched the same recipe in a similar can next year, why would your buyer still choose yerby?
- Who sells a natural or yerba mate energy drink within reach of your buyer today, at what price? (/founder-competitors)
- How do your first 100 paying customers arrive, through which channel, and what does each one cost to acquire? (/founder-consumer, /founder-cfo)
- What is your wholesale price, and what is left per can after a café, distributor or chain takes its cut? (/founder-cfo)
- What does the can deliver that matters to an energy-drink buyer (caffeine per can, taste, effect), and is it clearly better than the alternative, or only cleaner?
- In ten years, what is the durable asset: the brand, a café network, a category name? How would you start building it with this first batch?
- How much time will each founder commit, and what cash is there beyond the first run?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
The vote is PASS, not FUND IF. These would move it to FUND IF:
1. **Name the beachhead.** A specific small market (a defined set of venues, a city, a buyer type) where yerby can plausibly become the default within a year, and the first 3,000 cans are sold into it rather than spread across four channels.
2. **Show channel economics that leave a profit.** From /founder-cfo: the wholesale price and the margin per can after channel cuts and delivery, at the planned volume, with acquisition cost from /founder-consumer for the first 100 customers.
3. **Show a reason it is not a commodity.** Either evidence from /founder-competitors that nobody owns this position within reach of the same buyer, or a concrete plan for the moat (brand built in one place first, exclusive venue relationships, a recipe or process the producer cannot reuse for others).
4. **Real repeat purchase.** Evidence from the first batch that venues reorder and buyers come back, since a brand moat only forms through repeat buying.
