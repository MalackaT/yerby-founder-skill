# Product lens
Score: 6 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **The product already fits in one sentence.** "Cold-brewed, carbonated yerba mate with nothing in it but water, mate and fruit." That is rare at pre-launch and it is the right starting point for this lens.
- **Focus on the product itself.** Four ingredients, one SKU, one can format. The ingredient list *is* the pitch, and nothing in the recipe has to be explained away.
- **Craft where customers cannot see it.** A 12-hour cold brew instead of extract or added caffeine, and no preservatives, are choices you only make if you care about the parts nobody sees. That is the kind of discipline this lens rewards.
- **Retail-ready details done early.** The final can design, matte finish, minimalist label and a proper GS1 EAN-13 barcode show attention to the touchpoint the buyer handles first.
- **A small first run is the right size to learn from.** 3,000 cans at ~24 CZK costs about 72,000 CZK in production. At the 55 CZK target shelf price that is roughly 31 CZK (about 56%) gross per can sold direct. At 79 CZK it is about 55 CZK (about 70%). Before distribution and marketing costs, the product has room for a margin.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **The first sip is not what the target customer expects.** The target is *mainstream energy-drink drinkers*, but the recipe has no added sweetener and no added caffeine. Unsweetened mate is earthy and bitter. The caffeine dose per can is not given. If the first five minutes feel less tasty and less energising than the drink they replace, the clean label will not save it, and a buyer who is disappointed once does not try it again. The brand's whole promise ("an energy alternative") depends on two facts that are missing: the caffeine per can and how this taste lands with this customer. A buyer panel (/founder-consumer) and a measured caffeine figure answer them.
2. **Too many channels for two founders and 3,000 cans.** D2C, cafés and bistros, wholesale distributors and later retail chains are four different experiences, with four different prices and four different hand-offs. Each hand-off is a seam where the product can break: warm or dented cans in a courier box, a café fridge stocking it behind the familiar brands, a distributor with no story to tell. Spreading 3,000 cans across all four means learning nothing well. The wholesale price, distributor margins and per-order shipping cost are not given. /founder-cfo answers whether each channel even works at a 55 CZK shelf price.
3. **The experience around the can does not yet match the can.** English-only labels for a Czech mainstream buyer, a social voice that fights both energy drinks *and* matcha (two enemies blur the one sentence), and an unknown shelf life for a pasteurised, preservative-free drink. Whether labels need Czech-language mandatory information, and how long the product holds its taste and fizz on a shelf, are open questions to answer before retail (/founder-ops). If the drink tastes flat or stale by month three, a lapse nobody sees becomes a lapse everyone tastes.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Say it in one sentence to a Red Bull or Monster drinker. Why do they switch, and what do they feel 20 minutes later?
- How much caffeine is in a can, and how does that compare to what your target buyer drinks now? (Do not guess. Measure it.)
- Have mainstream energy-drink drinkers, not mate fans or friends, tasted it blind? What share wanted a second can? (/founder-consumer)
- Of D2C, cafés, distributors and chains, which one channel gets the first 3,000 cans, and what do you say no to?
- Describe the first five minutes for a D2C buyer: the ad, the checkout, the box arriving, the first cold sip. Who owns each step, and what does shipping cost per order? (/founder-cfo)
- What is the tested shelf life, and how do taste and carbonation hold up at month 1, 3 and 6?
- Why fight matcha as well? Who is the one enemy?
- Is mandatory label information legally compliant for sale in Czechia as printed?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Prove the first sip.** Before the full run ships, a blind taste test with real mainstream energy-drink drinkers, plus a stated caffeine figure per can. If most of them do not want a second can, change the recipe or the target customer before you spend on reach.
2. **One channel for the first batch.** Pick the single channel where the founders can control the whole experience (for example, a handful of cafés they serve themselves, or D2C to the email list), run the 3,000 cans through it, and cut the rest until that channel works.
3. **Close the gaps between can and shelf.** Confirm label compliance and a tested shelf life before any distributor or retail conversation. Narrow the social voice to one enemy and one sentence.
4. **Numbers per channel before scaling.** The /founder-cfo step must show a positive margin per can in the chosen channel at the real wholesale or shipping cost, not only at the 55 CZK shelf price.
